# Transfer Assessment: Ukraine

Last Updated: October 16, 2026

This document is the general part of the information that FOP Tkach Oleksandr ("TUWA," "we") provides under Clause 14 of the standard contractual clauses (Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914) to a customer of Quasar Cloud that concludes them with us ("Customer"). It describes the transfer of Customer Personal Data to us, the laws and practices of Ukraine that may apply to it, and the measures we take. The Customer uses it, with its own information, to assess whether the clauses can be complied with. Terms are used as defined in our Data Processing Addendum ("DPA").

## 2. Laws of Ukraine that may apply

This section describes the legal framework. The facts it confirms do not by themselves show that the protection of a particular flow is effective; the assessment of each flow (Section 5) applies them to that flow.

**Data protection.** The Law of Ukraine "On Personal Data Protection" (No. 2297-VI of 1 June 2010) governs the processing of personal data in Ukraine, and the Ukrainian Parliament Commissioner for Human Rights supervises compliance with it. Ukraine has ratified the Council of Europe Convention 108 and its Additional Protocol (Law No. 2438-VI of 6 July 2010). Ukraine is not covered by an adequacy decision of the European Commission.

**Access by public authorities.** Ukrainian authorities can request or obtain data held by a person in Ukraine through several procedures. Their conditions, and the control over them, differ:

- Criminal proceedings under the Criminal Procedure Code of Ukraine (2012). It has distinct procedures with different conditions: temporary access to things and documents, including electronic data (Articles 159–166); searches (Articles 233–236); and covert investigative actions, including obtaining information from electronic information systems (Articles 258–264). These actions are, as a rule, taken on a ruling of an investigating judge. For entering a home or other property there is a limited urgent exception, after which the prosecution must apply for a ruling of the investigating judge (Article 233(3), as amended by Law No. 720-IX). Section IX-1 (in particular Article 615, as amended by Law No. 2201-IX) sets special procedures for martial law where an investigating judge cannot act; they change the procedure in those cases and do not abolish judicial control in general.
- Operational and search activities (Law "On Operational and Search Activities", No. 2135-XII), for which that Law requires a court decision for the measures that interfere with the privacy of communications and data that it lists (Article 8), and the powers of the Security Service of Ukraine (Law "On the Security Service of Ukraine", No. 2229-XII), which are exercised within that Law and the rules of criminal procedure, not as a separate exception from them.
- Martial law, in force since 24 February 2022 (Presidential Decree No. 64/2022) and in force on the date of this document (Decree No. 596/2026). The decree allows the temporary restriction of the rights under Articles 30–34, 38, 39, 41–44 and 53 of the Constitution of Ukraine, within the measures of the legal regime of martial law (Article 64 of the Constitution and the Law "On the Legal Regime of Martial Law"). This list names the rights that may be restricted; it does not by itself set a procedure for access to data.

This is an overview of the procedures, not a statement of every power, urgent ground and exception in the current wording of these laws.

**Electronic communications.** The Law "On Electronic Communications" (No. 1089-IX) places obligations, including obligations towards authorities, on providers of electronic communications networks and services. Quasar Cloud processes information about blockchain transactions for the Customer's apps and sends the results to the Customer's webhook endpoints over internet connections that others provide. On these functions it does not appear to be an internet access service or an interpersonal communications service only because it uses a network. This is a preliminary view based on the functions described in Section 1, not a final qualification of every current and future function; we review it when the functions change (Section 6).

**Remedies.** Persons affected can challenge the actions of authorities in court and complain to the Ukrainian Parliament Commissioner for Human Rights; Ukraine is a party to the European Convention on Human Rights, so an application to the European Court of Human Rights is possible after domestic remedies. Their effectiveness in a particular case cannot be assumed, in particular where access is covert and the person concerned is not told of it.

## 3. Our practice

- The operator of TUWA confirms that, as of the date of this document, we have not received any request from a public authority of Ukraine or of any other country for Customer Personal Data. This is a statement of our practice, not a legal conclusion.
- We will publish a change to this section if that changes, as far as the law allows.
- Our experience is limited (Quasar Cloud is a free public beta and has no customers yet) and does not by itself show what authorities may do.

## 4. Our commitments and measures

- **If we receive a request** for Customer Personal Data from a public authority, we act as Clause 15 of the clauses requires: we notify the Customer, unless the law forbids it, and then seek a waiver of the prohibition; we review the legality of the request and challenge it where there are reasonable grounds; we disclose only the minimum needed; and we document the request, our assessment and our answer.
- **Location and access.** The data is stored in the Netherlands. Access is limited to the operator of TUWA, whose accounts use two-factor authentication; every record is scoped to its organization. The operator reads Customer Personal Data in the admin panel, which records each view (who, which records, when) for 1 year; a view that cannot be recorded is refused. There is no web tool for the database: it is reached only over SSH with the keys of the operator, for deployments and migrations, backups and restores, incidents and work the admin panel cannot do. Requests to the admin panel pass through our reverse proxy, which logs each request (time, path and status, not the data) for 7 days. Every hour an automated check copies these views, the SSH sign-ins of the servers and the requests to the admin panel into the review of the week and alerts us at once to a sign-in or a view it does not expect; every week we review them and record the reason of each SSH session and the data read. These records show what was viewed; they are not a measure against access to the disks of the servers. We do not export Customer Personal Data from the servers, except in the encrypted backups stored with Cloudflare R2.
- **Encryption.** TLS for every connection; stored secrets, and the keys sent with transactions, encrypted with AES-256-GCM; database backups encrypted before they leave our servers (DPA, Annex B). The data of transactions is processed in plain text, because the service needs to read it.
- **Minimisation.** Quasar Cloud needs public blockchain data; the Customer controls the free fields and must not send special categories. Webhook delivery logs are kept for up to 3 months; other data while the app that sent it exists (DPA, Section 10).
- **No back doors of our own.** We have not created back doors or similar means of access to Customer Personal Data, and we do not change our processing to facilitate access by authorities. Whether a law could require such means from TUWA depends on how its service is qualified (Section 2).

## 5. The assessment of each flow

This document is the general part of the assessment. It does not conclude for any particular Customer. For each flow, our signatory assesses how this general part applies to it before the package of standard contractual clauses is prepared: the categories of data the Customer's apps send, including what they put in free fields such as titles, descriptions and payloads, the webhook events and responses kept, the paths by which the data is stored and accessed, our access to it in plain text from Ukraine and the measures that apply. The signatory then writes our conclusion with the reasons of that flow, including why the measures are enough for its data, the ways it is accessed and the laws that apply; that no authority has asked for data so far does not decide it. The outcome is that the flow can proceed, that it needs additional measures that the package names, or that we cannot conclude the clauses for it. Additional measures are in place before the data of the flow is transferred: the Customer confirms it when it signs, and our signatory checks it, for example on the data actually received, and records how before we sign. The conclusion and the date of its next review are part of Annex I.B of the package. The Customer makes its own assessment and signs the package only if it agrees, or declines it with the reason; before we sign, our signatory confirms that the facts have not changed. That the wallet addresses are public does not by itself make free fields or our administrative access low-risk; each flow is assessed on what it actually contains.

## 6. Review

We review this assessment at least once a year and when the relevant law or practice changes, including the end of martial law, a new data protection law, a request from an authority, or a change in where or how we process Customer Personal Data. A new version is shown in the Quasar dashboard; a version that needs the Customer's acceptance is marked so, as described in the DPA.
